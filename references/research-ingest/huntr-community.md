## ⚠ PLATFORM-CRITICAL CONTEXT: HUNTR 2.0 PIVOT (verified 2026-08-15)

- **2026-06-08**: huntr announced Huntr 2.0 — evolution from a traditional OSS bug-bounty platform into a **challenge-based competition arena for AI security research** ("Stop Writing Reports, Start Breaking AI", blog.huntr.com/huntr-2-0-stop-writing-reports-start-breaking-ai; FAQ: blog.huntr.com/huntr-2-0-faq). Launch day June 12, 2026 with leaderboard.
- **OSS vulnerability program sunset**: stopped accepting new OSS submissions **2026-06-30**; all OSS submissions **locked 2026-07-31**; after that the program is frozen, unprocessed submissions stay private, and **no further bounties are issued**. OSS maintainers directed to GitHub Security Advisories.
- **MFV program**: paused/kept "whilst we see the results of the launch", with plans to transition it to challenge-based format; the huntr.com/bounties page shows "This page is sunsetting soon" (Model File Formats: 56 programs listed).
- **Challenge-mode economics**: structured challenges on environments huntr controls (e.g., "Inside Job - Three Agents, Three Secrets" — $15,000 — make three AI agents spill protected tools/secrets; "Hack AskNova - the Stock Trading Agent" — extract secrets + perform unauthorized trades, closed 2026-07-12). Rewards for execution in challenge environments, not report triage; faster feedback loops by design.
- **Implication for this cluster**: any concept below describing pre-2.0 huntr economics (payout tiers, multipliers, caps, disclosure timelines, verdict statistics) is **OUTDATED context** even where the underlying hunting *methodology* survives. Methodology concepts transfer to challenge mode (objective-based attacks on AI agents/models, leaderboard pressure) but their platform framing does not.

## CONCEPTS

### Operating philosophy
- [philosophy] **Impact-tier over bug-count**: top hunters deliberately chase RCE, DoS, sandbox escape, and arbitrary file read/write on important systems; "I'm not trying to find the most bugs — I aim to find meaningful, difficult vulnerabilities" (taiphung217). Select hard, high-visibility AI/ML targets (Transformers, Ollama, PyTorch, TensorFlow, MLflow) and set monthly goals rather than spraying many easy targets. [superseded-risk: target list pre-dates the 2.0 pivot; challenge-mode targets are curated challenge environments + MFVs, not arbitrary OSS repos]
- [philosophy] **Escalate-before-report**: taiphung217 sat on a found path traversal for two extra days to escalate to arbitrary file write, then RCE through a file-writing feature (5-day total). The leaderboard rewards max impact per report; report at the highest provable impact level, not the first primitive. [transferable: challenge scoring likewise rewards max demonstrated impact per environment]
- [philosophy] **Duplicate tolerance as a signal**: winters0x64 opened with a string of duplicates on AnythingLLM before a first valid bug; community consensus is that duplicates mean you are in the right area — persistence past them is the standard entry cost. [superseded-risk: duplicate dynamics differ in challenge mode (fixed challenge environments, first-to-break scoring)]
- [philosophy] **Platform trend-following for research direction**: Lyutoon and taiphung217 both treat huntr's program updates (MFV initiative, new targets) as a map of where bounty money and undiscovered attack surface currently live — "updates align with current trends and provide direction for identifying new research targets." [transferable: huntr 2.0 challenge list + "Behind the Bypass" content is the new map]
- [philosophy] **Persistence through rejection**: zpbrent's pickle findings were first judged "informative" before refined PoCs flipped them to critical (2 CVEs, CVE-2023-6730 at 9.0 CVSS); m0kr4n3 similarly pivoted after a run of informative verdicts on non-web vectors (privilege-escalation-style findings were not accepted; web vectors were). Refine PoC + demonstrate impact instead of abandoning. [superseded-risk: "informative" verdict pipeline is pre-2.0; in challenge mode the verdict is binary (environment broken/not)]
- [philosophy] **Hard targets compound learning + ranking**: 5 months of consistent, high-impact-focused effort took taiphung217 from newcomer to quarterly #1; the all-time board remains the longer game. [transferable: 2.0 leaderboard preserves the ranking incentive]

### Workflow habits (methodology)
- [methodology] **Docs-first, then source**: read the application documentation to understand intended behavior, identify overlooked/interesting functionality, then read the source of *those* functions — never read the codebase from scratch (winters0x64, taiphung217). [transferable]
- [methodology] **1-day variant hunting as primary technique**: study existing reports/PoCs on the same project, understand root cause context, then search the codebase for sibling instances or deeper flaws in the same mechanism (taiphung217; also the Big Sleep variant-analysis pattern echoed in johnmatrix). MLflow's bug family (auth-less artifact write, model-versions LFI, HTTP dataset source RCE) is the canonical worked example — one root-cause family produced CVEs by kevin-mizu, haxatron, McInerney, taiphung217. [transferable; superseded-risk: variant corpus shifts from OSS hacktivity to challenge disclosures + "Behind the Bypass" zero-day writeups]
- [methodology] **Split manual/automated workflow**: manual doc-driven edge-case/logic analysis + automation with Joern (taint/query) and AFL++ (fuzzing) (taiphung217). [transferable]
- [methodology] **Fuzzing-first, audit when fuzzing is infeasible**: Lyutoon fuzzes malformed GGUF model files to find Ollama crashes; switches to manual code audit for logic flaws fuzzing can't reach, noting audited bugs "often have greater impact or value." [transferable to MFV/challenge environments]
- [technique] **Fork + CodeQL Action for free static analysis**: fork the target repo, add a CodeQL workflow, read results in the fork's Security tab — surfaces code paths and low-hanging fruit with zero local tooling (winters0x64). [transferable only where targets are OSS; challenge environments are huntr-controlled — re-verify what is accessible]
- [technique] **Heat-map codebase hotspots**: prioritize file handling (upload/download/artifact processing → LFI, arbitrary overwrite), auth logic (login/session → ATO), and memory management (C/C++ parsing → overflow) (McInerney). MLflow LFI found via artifact operations is his worked example. [transferable]
- [technique] **Sanitizer/validation bypass as first-class strategy**: after reading source, attack validation code and sanitizers for bypasses (winters0x64); m0kr4n3's stored XSS in open-webui came from digging into sanitization processes others had already "cleaned." [transferable]
- [methodology] **New-repo monitoring for dup avoidance**: watch for fresh targets and submit early before the crowd (m0kr4n3) — the inverse of the 1-day-variant strategy; both are dup-management tactics. [superseded-risk: pre-2.0; challenge environments are fixed, so "new repo" timing becomes "new challenge release" timing]
- [methodology] **Hacktivity as training corpus**: study the platform's disclosed reports (hacktivity page) to learn which attack classes actually work in the wild instead of OWASP theory (McInerney, MLsecops podcast). [superseded-risk: hacktivity corpus frozen 2026-07-31; new corpus = 2.0 challenge writeups + "Behind the Bypass" series]
- [methodology] **Re-hunt scraped projects**: hainguyen0207 found a path traversal in LOLLMS after "many researchers had already explored the project" — deep re-reads of heavily hunted codebases still yield bugs when others move on. [transferable]

### Tooling
- [tool] **Tool stack per hunter**: taiphung217 — Joern, AFL++; winters0x64 — CodeQL (via GitHub Actions on forks); Lyutoon — fuzzing + differential testing across DL frameworks (TensorFlow, PyTorch, Paddle, MindSpore; USENIX Sec 2023 work); McInerney — Snyk for coarse triage (99% warnings, but flags dense areas), Burp Suite for web, LLMs for bypass brainstorming, AFL++ structure-aware fuzzing + GDB + ASAN for model loaders. [transferable]
- [tool] **MFV hunting kit**: search file-parsing functions (header parsing, memory allocation); test extreme header values (max/zero); look for integer overflows, unchecked array access, blind trust in header values (McInerney MFV guide). [transferable — MFV program still the open/pre-challenge-format channel as of 2026-08]
- [tool] **Scanner-evasion awareness**: huntr's own ModelScan-based scanner now catches classic archive-slip and symlink payloads; hunters must use nested/encrypted/obfuscated variants to reach the high-value tier (huntr blog, 2025-05-15). [superseded-risk: scanner state 2026 unverified; evasion depth needed in challenge environments may differ]

### Model-format economics (what pays) — [superseded-risk: all pre-2.0 payout framing is OUTDATED context]
- [technique] **Archive-slip as pivot, not payoff**: model formats (.nemo, .keras, .gguf, .pth) are zip/tar archives; raw `extractall()` yields write-anywhere — dead on its own for bounties (scanner catches `../../` and stray symlinks), but the fastest path to load-time ACE, backdoors, and output tampering. [technique survives; bounty framing doesn't]
- [technique] **Symlink archive members beat naive path filters**: loaders that strip `../` still extract symlink members pointing outside the sandbox (huntr blog PoC pattern). [technique survives]
- [philosophy] **Payout gradient shapes target choice**: high-value = .safetensors/.gguf/.keras/.joblib, load-time code execution via header/metadata abuse, backdoors/output manipulation, scanner-evading payloads; low/medium = .pkl and fringe formats, plain Zip/TarSlip, DoS via oversized tensors, bugs needing extra imports. Bounty tiers: MFV beta up to $1,500 ($750 for pickle), later posts cite up to $3–4k; critical OSV caps at $50k (Hugging Face Transformers inaugural $50k top). **[OUTDATED — pre-2.0 economics; superseded by challenge-based rewards; re-verify MFV rewards under the 2026-08 program status]**

### Disclosure & economics (platform-level) — [superseded-risk: pre-2.0 economics]
- [methodology] **Disclosure pipeline knowledge for timing**: maintainer gets 31 days to respond; valid reports get CVE + public disclosure on day 90; informative/invalid go public immediately; MFV reports are not publicly disclosed. Review turnaround averages 85.7 days (Ayala & Garcia). **[OUTDATED — OSV pipeline frozen 2026-07-31; day-90/CVE-assignment pipeline dead; CVE assignment for OSS now via GHSA]**
- [philosophy] **10× multiplier steers research**: reports affecting ML model read/write (reading/writing models or training data) get bounty multipliers up to 10× — hunt impact framed in model/training-data terms, not just server compromise. **[OUTDATED — pre-2.0; "model/training-data impact framing" as a prioritization heuristic still transfers to MFV/challenge scoring]**
- [research finding] **Empirics (Ayala & Garcia, S&P 2025)**: of 6,427 huntr reports (09/2023–02/2025): AI/ML OSS vulns skew High severity; CWE mix diverges from OWASP ML Top 10 (e.g., CWE-400 DoS); 44.4% of CVE-IDs missing from NVD (alert gap); 49.5% of severity-assigned AI/ML vulns remain unfixed post-disclosure vs 99.7% fix rate for non-AI/ML; AI/ML reports pay more but draw more Informative/N/A verdicts (reviewer skepticism). **[OUTDATED — historical baseline of the pre-2.0 platform; NVD-gap finding remains a current `vuln-intel` design input]**
- [economics] **AI-slop counter-pressure (2026)**: curl shut its HackerOne program (Jan 2026) over AI-generated reports; invalid/duplicate rates rose to ~50–65%, triage time 8–14h; platforms respond with AI triage (HackerOne Hai Triage) and stricter PoC/video-evidence requirements — raising the bar for reproducible, evidence-backed reports. [CURRENT — 2026-07-24 johnmatrix; directly motivates the huntr 2.0 pivot to challenge format]
- [methodology] **AI-augmented expert workflow (2026 state)**: effective hunters use AI to build code-comprehension (50k-line data-flow mapping, auth-boundary mapping) and generate hypotheses, but keep human target selection + validation; the Big Sleep/SHERPA lineage shows variant analysis of recent commits is the strongest AI fit. Structured tool exposure (MCP-based frameworks, e.g., PentestMCP; methodology-gated agent skills like Claude Code bug-bounty skill) roughly doubles methodology adherence vs unconstrained LLMs. [CURRENT — 2026-07-24 johnmatrix]
